# BlueProof field photograph protocol

Version 0.1, 18 September 2026. For monitors, and for the evaluation dataset.

## Why this document exists

The published evidence on vision models in the field is blunt: performance
collapses on real field imagery compared with clean reference photographs, and
the dominant cause is **image legibility**, not the difficulty of telling
species apart. That finding is good news, because legibility is something a
protocol can fix and taxonomy is not.

Everything below is designed to close that gap. Follow it and the model has a
chance. Ignore it and no amount of model quality rescues the submission.

---

## The five shots per plot

Take all five. It takes about ninety seconds once you are used to it.

**1. The plot marker.** Photograph the plot code tag or marker post so the code
is readable in the frame. This ties every other shot to a known plot.

**2. The wide shot.** Stand back far enough that the whole plot is in frame,
camera roughly chest height, looking slightly down. This shows density, spacing
and the general state of the ground.

**3. The close shot.** One representative plant filling most of the frame,
camera about one arm's length away. Leaves and the root structure should both
be visible. This is the shot the species assessment depends on.

**4. The leaf shot.** A single leaf cluster, close, in focus, with the
underside visible if you can turn one over. Leaf shape, tip and colour carry
most of the identification signal.

**5. The problem shot.** Anything wrong: a cut stump, axe marks, boring holes,
frass, dieback, rubbish, a blocked channel. If nothing is wrong, photograph the
healthiest plant instead and say so.

---

## Rules that decide whether the photo is usable

**Light.** Mid-morning or mid-afternoon. Avoid noon, which flattens everything
into glare, and avoid dusk. Keep the sun behind you, not behind the plant.

**Tide.** Low or falling tide, so roots and ground are visible. A plot
photographed at high water shows you almost nothing.

**Focus.** Tap the screen on the plant before you shoot. Check the photo before
you walk away. If it is blurred, take it again on the spot; you will not come
back.

**Framing.** Fill the frame with the subject. A seedling occupying five percent
of the image is not assessable.

**Steady and level.** Both hands, elbows in. Hold still for a second after the
shutter.

**Do not.** No zoom, walk closer instead. No filters or beauty modes. No flash
in daylight. Do not clean up, prune or replant before photographing.

---

## What must be switched on

Location services on, before you start walking. Date and time correct. Take the
photographs through the BlueProof app or the messaging flow rather than the
camera roll, so that position, time and your identity attach to the image at
capture. **A photograph without provenance is a picture, not evidence.** A
carbon buyer is purchasing the chain of custody, not the scenery.

---

## Reporting honestly

You are paid for a verified submission, not for a surviving seedling.

Say what you see. If half the plot has died, photograph the dead half and report
it. Dead seedlings reported accurately are still paid work, and accurate loss
data is worth more to this project than a flattering number. Nothing about your
payment improves if the plot looks better than it is.

If you are unsure of the species, record what you believe and mark it uncertain.
Uncertainty is information. A confident wrong answer is the expensive kind.

## For the evaluation dataset specifically

If these photographs are contributing to the evaluation described in the paper,
three additional requirements apply.

**Capture conditions must be ordinary.** Do not schedule collection for a
perfect day with the best handset. The purpose is to measure performance under
the conditions that will actually obtain, so a corpus gathered under favourable
conditions is worse than useless: it produces a flattering number that will not
survive deployment.

**Every image needs an independent expert label.** Species, condition and
presence of disturbance, established by a trained botanist at the plot at the
time of capture, not afterwards from the photograph. A label derived from the
same image being tested is not ground truth.

**Duplicate a subset.** Have two people label the same one hundred images
independently so inter-rater agreement can be reported. This tells you the
ceiling imposed by label noise, which is the number that says how good any model
could possibly look.

Target roughly one thousand images across Tudor Creek and Gazi Bay. One hundred
is enough to find out whether the idea works at all.

---

## Consent and privacy

Do not photograph people without asking. If someone is in the frame
incidentally, and they object, delete it. Agree with the community forest
association before collection starts on who can see the images, how long they
are kept, and what happens to them if the project ends. Settle this before
collection, not after.
